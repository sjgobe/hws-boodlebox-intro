Advancing AI Literacy at HWS

Generative AI at HWS begins with a community conversation.

Before BoodleBox was a platform decision, it was a response to what faculty, staff, and students were already discussing: how generative AI is changing teaching, learning, assessment, privacy, and access.

This introduction is rooted in two questions: What were we hearing as a community? And why did BoodleBox emerge as the right space to address those concerns while moving AI literacy forward?

Start with the conversation Why BoodleBox?

The original framing

Our conversations in Spring and Summer 2025 kept returning to the same themes. We needed a way to engage AI directly without losing sight of critical thinking, transparency, privacy, equity, and responsible teaching practice.

The First Big Idea

What we were talking about as a community

Across faculty and staff conversations, the issue was never simply whether AI existed. The question was how HWS should respond thoughtfully to a tool that students were already using and that was already reshaping academic work.

The concerns we kept naming

  • Critical thinking concerns — how do we support student thinking rather than replace it?
  • Students overusing the tool — where is the line between support and dependence?
  • Not understanding how LLMs work — how do we build real AI literacy, not just casual use?
  • Students using it and not talking about it — how do we create space for open, honest discussion?

The teaching questions underneath them

  • Privacy of data — what should and should not be entered into AI systems?
  • Changing teaching practices — what do best practices look like now?
  • Assessment — how should assignments evolve when AI can assist with drafting, summarizing, and generating?
  • Environmental impact and cost — how do we use these tools intentionally and equitably?
The Second Big Idea

Why BoodleBox addresses those conversations

BoodleBox was not chosen as a generic AI tool. It was chosen because it provides a structured, protected, and collaborative environment where HWS can engage the exact issues raised by the community.

A better environment for responsible AI use

  • Multi-model access helps users compare outputs rather than treating one answer as authoritative.
  • Equitable access for students means AI literacy does not depend on personal paid subscriptions.
  • FERPA/HIPAA-compliant design supports institutional expectations around privacy and data protection.
  • Does not train on user data helps create a safer space for academic experimentation.
  • Anonymized prompts add another layer of protection for users and institutional context.

A platform for teaching and learning, not just prompting

  • AI literacy forward means using the platform to teach discernment, not just efficiency.
  • Teach prompting as a communicative and critical skill.
  • Share conversations so faculty and students can examine AI interactions together.
  • Collaborative aspect supports discussion, reflection, and community learning.
  • Classroom assignments can be built with AI use in mind rather than avoiding the topic entirely.
Moving Forward

What AI literacy can look like at HWS

The goal is not to encourage uncritical AI adoption. The goal is to help our community engage these tools with more clarity, more transparency, and stronger judgment.

Understand

How these tools work

Build shared understanding of what large language models can do well, where they fail, and why their outputs require human review.

Practice

Better prompting and better questioning

Treat prompting as part of inquiry: give context, ask for revision, compare responses, and reflect on what the system is actually producing.

Evaluate

Outputs critically

Use AI as a thinking partner rather than a final authority by checking claims, analyzing bias, and bringing human expertise back into the process.

Discuss

Use openly

Create more honest conversations about how and when students are using AI, instead of leaving that use hidden or undefined.

Design

Assignments and assessments intentionally

Use AI’s presence as an opportunity to revisit process, reflection, authorship, and what meaningful evidence of learning looks like.

Support

Equitable and responsible adoption

Keep cost, privacy, and environmental impact in view while helping all members of the HWS community develop AI literacy together.
